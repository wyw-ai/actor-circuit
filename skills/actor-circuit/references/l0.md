## Scope and provenance

L0 is a practical vocabulary for stable, business-independent collaboration-state transitions. It is not intended to be a mathematically minimal instruction set or a closed standard.

The underlying structures are not original to this project. Sequence, branching, parallel split, synchronization, partial joins, races, cancellation, loops, and termination have long histories in workflow and distributed-systems research. Cross-participant distinctions also draw from service-interaction research and contemporary Agent frameworks.

This project reorganizes those ideas around a shared Actor abstraction, informed by Multi-Actor engineering practice, and separates them from L1 communication and L2 system composition.

Primary antecedents include:

- [Workflow Patterns](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/workflow-patterns-2/), van der Aalst et al., 2003.
- [Workflow Control-Flow Patterns](https://www.workflowpatterns.com/patterns/control/), Workflow Patterns Initiative.
- [Service Interaction Patterns](https://kodu.ut.ee/~dumas/ServiceInteractionPatterns/documentation.html), Barros, Dumas, and ter Hofstede, 2005.

## Core concepts

### Actor

An Actor is an active, independently identifiable collaboration boundary that can receive input, advance state within its boundary, and produce an observable outcome.

An Actor may be a Human, AI Agent, Service instance, Script execution, Team, Workflow instance, or External System. Intelligence and use of an LLM are not requirements.

A static definition is not an active Actor by itself:

| Static definition | Active collaboration boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Service code and API | A Service instance receiving work and producing outcomes |
| Workflow definition | A Workflow instance that can wait, resume, and terminate |
| Script file | A Script execution with concrete input and outcome |
| Agent prompt | A running Agent with a concrete lifecycle |

Actor boundaries are recursive. A Team or Workflow can appear as one Actor externally while containing a circuit of Actors internally.

### Work

Work is the collaboration subject whose state and ability to continue are changed by gates. It may represent a task, request, decision, investigation, approval, conversation, or another bounded unit of progress.

### Branch

A Branch is an independently advancing work path created or selected within a circuit. A branch is not merely a message recipient: it carries work state and has an eventual relationship to convergence, cancellation, or termination.

### Continuation

A Continuation is the right and obligation to determine or perform the next transition for a work path. It is distinct from executing the current operation.

Three relationships must remain distinguishable:

- **Execution:** who performs the current work.
- **Continuation:** who owns the next transition.
- **Observation:** who may see the process or outcome.

### Gate

A Collaboration Logic Gate is a stable, business-independent, composable, and observable collaboration-state transition.

A useful gate has a recognizable enabling condition and outcome. It may still be decomposable. Retry, Race, and Quorum are retained because they carry stable engineering semantics even though they can be expressed with more elementary control flow.

### Circuit

A Circuit is a composition of gates and Actor boundaries that describes how work can progress, split, converge, wait, change hands, repeat, and terminate.

A diagram showing connected boxes is only a topology. It becomes a meaningful circuit when the relevant gate semantics are explicit.

## Gate vocabulary

### Progress gates

#### Sequence

Enable a successor only after its predecessor reaches the required completion state.

`A → B` means completion of A enables B. Dispatching A does not imply completion of A.

Sequence defines ordering, not which Actor executes either step or how information is transported.

#### Branch

Select one or more successor paths according to current state or an observed result.

The decision may come from a rule, Human, Agent, or External System. Branch defines the effect of the decision on continuations, not how the decision is computed.

#### Route

Select an Actor to receive or continue work according to capability, content, state, policy, or availability.

Route chooses an Actor. It does not guarantee discovery, delivery, acceptance, or a transfer of continuation ownership.

#### Stop

End a collaboration scope with an explicit terminal status, such as succeeded, failed, cancelled, rejected, or exhausted.

Silence and lack of visible activity are not terminal facts.

### Split and convergence gates

#### Fan-out

Expand one work path into multiple branches that may advance independently.

Common intentions include partitioning different subproblems, replicating the same problem, or diversifying models, tools, roles, or strategies.

Fan-out permits independent progress; it does not require simultaneous execution. It also does not imply broadcast: visibility of a message is not assignment of work.

#### Fan-in

Use the states of multiple branches to enable one successor path after a declared convergence condition is satisfied.

Convergence may wait for all declared branches, the first acceptable branch, a quorum, or the best available result at a deadline or budget boundary.

Fan-in must make the relevant branch set, convergence condition, dynamic-membership rule, reset behavior, and late-result policy understandable.

Fan-in decides **when and from which branches** the circuit converges. Aggregation, reduction, voting, selection, and synthesis decide **how branch content is processed**.

#### Race

Let multiple branches compete to satisfy the same continuation condition, with a declared rule for choosing the winner.

Race must also define the fate of non-winning and near-simultaneous branches: cancel, ignore, preserve, continue as fallback, or resolve by deterministic priority.

Race is often implemented as specialized Fan-in behavior, but its winner and loser semantics justify naming it independently.

#### Quorum

Enable a successor when a declared number or weight of branch conditions has been satisfied.

Quorum defines the threshold, eligible population, membership changes, and treatment of remaining branches. It does not define how opinions or evidence are produced.

### Actor-boundary gates

#### Call

Ask another Actor to perform work while retaining the caller's continuation. Completion is expected to return to the caller's existing continuation.

`A → B → A`

The callee may contain an arbitrarily complex internal circuit without owning the caller's outer continuation.

#### Delegate

Assign a bounded unit of work to another Actor, making that Actor its executor and expected delivery owner.

Delegate changes execution ownership. Whether the delegator retains final accountability, whether redelegation is allowed, and how budget or permissions propagate are not determined by the gate.

#### Transfer

Move continuation ownership for a work path to another Actor.

`A → B → …`

After Transfer, the previous Actor is no longer a required continuation point. A complete handoff normally includes Transfer plus L1 context carriage, acceptance, and recovery semantics.

#### Return

Resolve a pre-existing Call or Delegate boundary by delivering an observable outcome to its declared continuation point.

Outcomes may include completed, partially completed, failed, rejected, or blocked. Ceasing output is not Return.

### Time and repetition gates

#### Wait

Suspend a work path until a declared event, condition, Actor outcome, Human decision, or time becomes observable.

Wait preserves a future continuation. It is neither failure nor termination.

#### Timeout

Produce a timeout outcome when a declared temporal boundary is crossed.

Timeout is a fact available to a successor Branch; it does not inherently mean failure. It may lead to cancellation, degradation, Retry, Route, Transfer, Human intervention, or Stop.

#### Retry

Reattempt a declared operation under bounded conditions.

Retry semantics include the target operation, retryable outcomes, attempt or budget limit, delay or backoff, and treatment of effects from earlier attempts.

Retry is a failure-conditioned local Loop, but its relationship to idempotency and side effects makes it independently useful.

#### Loop

Repeat a circuit region until a declared termination condition is satisfied.

A Loop requires an observable condition and a bound on rounds, time, cost, or another resource. A model's belief that further improvement is possible is not by itself a sufficient bound.

#### Resume

Reactivate a waiting, suspended, or interrupted work path from its preserved collaboration state when a declared trigger occurs.

Resume restores the work path, not necessarily the original process, machine, model session, or Actor instance.

## Essential distinctions

| Do not conflate | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Fan-out / Broadcast | Fan-out creates work branches; Broadcast changes information visibility. |
| Fan-in / Reduce | Fan-in controls convergence; Reduce processes content. |
| Branch / Route | Branch selects successor paths; Route selects an Actor. |
| Route / Transfer | Route selects a destination; Transfer changes continuation ownership. |
| Call / Delegate | Call preserves a caller continuation; Delegate assigns bounded work ownership. |
| Delegate / Transfer | Delegate changes the executor; Transfer changes the continuation owner. |
| Wait / Stop | Wait preserves a future continuation; Stop creates a terminal state. |
| Race / Voting | Race controls which branch may continue; Voting is one possible decision mechanism. |
| Gate / Circuit | A gate is one stable transition; a circuit composes transitions. |
| Circuit / Pattern | A circuit describes collaboration flow; a pattern resolves recurring forces and includes consequences. |

## Semantic completeness

A circuit need not answer every possible engineering question, but it must not hide a question that changes its collaboration semantics.

Relevant completeness conditions include:

- **Observable progress:** Enabling and completion conditions are distinguishable from dispatch and silence.
- **Explicit continuation:** The next path or continuation owner is knowable after each transition.
- **Work ownership:** Execution ownership and continuation ownership are distinguishable where they diverge.
- **Branch identity:** A Fan-out creates an understandable set of branches and a policy for their eventual convergence, cancellation, preservation, or independent termination.
- **Convergence:** Fan-in, Race, and Quorum declare membership, thresholds, late results, and remaining-branch behavior where relevant.
- **Bounded suspension:** Wait exposes what can resume, time out, cancel, or otherwise resolve it.
- **Bounded repetition:** Retry and Loop expose termination and resource bounds.
- **Explicit transfer:** Transfer exposes the new continuation owner; reliable acceptance belongs to L1.
- **Explicit termination:** Stop produces a terminal fact rather than relying on inactivity.

These are semantic questions, not a required analysis checklist or output schema. Apply them where they materially affect the system under discussion.

## What L0 does not define

L0 does not define:

- Actor identifiers, discovery, addressing, or routing tables;
- message or artifact formats;
- delivery, acknowledgement, ordering, duplication, or idempotency mechanisms;
- correlation and causation representation;
- durable storage, offline behavior, resumption transport, or recovery;
- permissions, trust, budgets, or delegation policy;
- result aggregation algorithms;
- complete Multi-Agent system compositions;
- collaboration design patterns and their consequences.

Those concerns motivate L1 and L2. An L0 analysis may expose them as dependencies without pretending to specify them.
